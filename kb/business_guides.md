# Business notes (STARTER - general guidance, replace with local data)
Dairy: income comes from daily milk sales. Main costs are feed, labour, electricity, transport and animal healthcare. Common risks are animal disease, rising feed prices, seasonal variation in milk and depending on a single buyer. Value added products such as curd, paneer and ghee can improve margins.

Retail: grocery and general shops have quick stock turnover but thin margins and need working capital for stock. Competition from nearby shops and low local purchasing power are the main risks. Home delivery can differentiate a shop.

Textile and tailoring: demand is often seasonal with festival and wedding peaks. Raw material cost and dependence on wholesale buyers are risks. Tailoring needs low investment and earns mainly on labour; school uniforms and alterations give steady work.

Food processing: local raw material and packaged food demand are strengths. Licences such as FSSAI registration may be needed, and spoilage and input price spikes are risks.

Poultry: production cycles are short, but disease outbreaks, heat stress and feed cost are major risks.

Competition: before opening, count similar businesses within 5 to 10 km. Many similar businesses means pricing pressure; none at all may mean there is little local demand, so check with a small village survey.
